## Design Rationale

### System Architecture

- Dedicated low-level motion controller
- Bare-metal firmware
- Hardware-assisted peripherals (DMA, timers, interrupts)
- Separation between high-level coordination and deterministic control

### PCB Layout

- Functional block separation
- Continuous ground plane
- Short routing of timing-critical signals
- Separation between logic and power electronics
- Modular board architecture
- DC-link capacitor placement: MLCC placed close to the switching MOSFETs (fast-transient response), electrolytics placed farther out via short, wide stub branches off the main current-return polygon (bulk reservoir role) — not equidistant from the switching node, since the two cap types serve different frequency ranges
- Current-return polygon reshaping: shorten path *length* (current-flow direction), not *width* (cross-section), for space savings — a length cut lowers resistance/inductance as a free side benefit, while a width cut needs a full ampacity check. Validated on HPS (shunt repositioned into an existing narrower polygon step, ~11mm height + ~13mm length reclaimed on that section without touching current-carrying cross-section)
- Shunt/busbar co-location: shunt stacked directly above its RedCube GND return terminal minimizes both trace length and Kelvin-sense loop area simultaneously
- HPS_A/B vs HPS_C/D floor plan: attempted separating the two HPS bridges with MPS Winch as a low-heat spacer (thermal isolation per ThermalBudget.md §6) plus relocating Steppers away from the gap; abandoned — routing corridor couldn't accommodate the signal count (22 Steppers + 30 remaining power-section signals) through the compacted layout. Current plan: accept HPS_A/B and HPS_C/D adjacent, mitigate the resulting thermal concentration with increased via density and larger connecting copper polygons to reduce spreading resistance into the rest of the board (not yet executed)
- MPS ring terminal reselected: Panduit EV14-6RB-Q (rated 16-14 AWG) was mismatched against the actual 18 AWG MPS wire gauge — replaced with Panduit PMV1-3RB-CY (M3 stud, properly 18 AWG rated). Deliberately not stud-matched to HPS's #6/M3.5 — sized for its own lighter duty instead
- HPS ring terminal (Molex 19071-0176) has an unresolved RedCube busbar clearance question — 3D fit-check showed ~1.5mm clearance on the un-squashed library model (comfortable margin at ≤48V, since required creepage is well under 1mm); a slimmer-sleeve alternative (Panduit, not yet found on Mouser) is also being evaluated
- **Space saving measure: finer signal routing.** Signal track width reduced from 0.200 mm to 0.150 mm, and the signal via changed from 0.620/0.310 mm to 0.505/0.200 mm (pad/drill, the same via as the thermal type), because the dense signal sections (Steppers bus, MCU fanout) need every square mm. The 0.620/0.310 mm via stays for power current-transfer; the other trace classes and the clearances are unchanged. The 0.200 mm drill is below the fab's standard minimum and carries an extra fee. Reflected in `PCBLayoutNotes.md` §5/§7.

### Component Selection

- **LPS gate resistor set to 10Ω** (source and sink), reusing a value already used elsewhere on the board rather than adding a distinct BOM line for a fifth resistor value. Checked against the 2EDN7533FXTMA1 driver's own peak output current rating (±5.0A typ, Table 16) before settling on it: PJA3406 (the LPS low-side MOSFET) publishes no internal gate resistance, unlike Winch/Heater's PJQ4548P-AU (1.6Ω) — so at the otherwise-standard 2Ω, the driver's minimum-resistance corner (RONSRC=0.4Ω/RONSNK=0.35Ω) computed to 5.06A/5.17A, exceeding the 5.0A peak rating outright. At 10Ω, worst-case current drops to ~1.17A (~23% of the rating) — comfortable margin. `ThermalBudget.md` §4.2 power figures are unaffected by this choice: LPS switching loss already used PJA3406's conservative datasheet bench tr/tf (39ns/28ns) rather than a gate-loop-derived estimate, and that stays the reference value (loop-derived times come out faster, not slower, for this part — see §4.2's Actual Gate Drive Loop note).
- **2Ω adopted as the standing default external gate resistor** (source and sink) across HPS and Winch, pending later revision (the Heater uses 10 Ω, see the end of this entry). Applying it to Winch/Heater's real gate-drive loop (UCC21551 for Winch, 2EDN7533FXTMA1 for Heater — a different driver family with its own RONSRC/RONSNK, no bootstrap/parallel-NMOS boost structure) barely moves their switching loss (Winch 0.029W→0.046W, Heater 0.007W→0.011W, combined +0.021W) — a sharp contrast to HPS's +1.28W, because Winch/Heater's PJQ4548P-AU has ~5.6x lower gate charge (13nC vs. HPS's 73nC) and correspondingly less charge to move even at the same loop resistance. Reflected in `ThermalBudget.md` §4.2.1/§4.2.2/§4.3/§5/§6 (board total was 21.793W at that point). The Heater gate resistor is 10 Ω instead, as on the LPS outputs: with Qg = 13 nC and about 12.4 Ω of total turn-on loop resistance it gives about 6 ns turn-on and about 23 ns turn-off, a peak gate current of about 1.15 A worst case (23% of the driver's 5 A rating) and an overdamped gate loop (critical series resistance about 9 Ω for an assumed 15 nH loop), at the cost of about 0.032 W switching loss at 12 V and 5 A instead of 0.011 W. Reflected in `ThermalBudget.md` §4.2.1/§5/§6: Heater total 0.260 W (was 0.239 W), board total 21.814 W.
- HPS gate drive resistor kept at **2Ω** (source and sink, single external resistor) despite the real switching-loss cost this carries once the actual gate-drive loop is accounted for (RGFET_Int=0.7Ω plus the UCC21551's own internal resistances push real tr/tf to 23.1ns/19.5ns, versus the MOSFET datasheet's idealized 3.5ns/5.7ns bench-test figures) — HPS switching loss rises from 0.353W to 1.636W, total HPS from 6.337W to 7.620W (reflected in `ThermalBudget.md` §4.3/§5/§6). A lower value (1.3Ω) was considered to partially offset RGFET_Int, but 2Ω was kept deliberately for its extra damping margin against turn-off/sink-phase ringing — the phase where ringing risks unintended re-turn-on and shoot-through in an H-bridge, versus turn-on ringing which mostly costs overshoot/EMI. This trade was affordable specifically because the earlier MOSFET reselection (ISC011N04NM7V) already freed up substantial thermal margin — HPS at 7.620W is still ~46% below the original 14.07W baseline.
- Gate_Supply (LM5164 auxiliary 12V buck) reworked from its original 68µH/300kHz design (a direct carryover of TI's reference-design current target, IOUT=1A from datasheet Fig 7-1) to **33µH/600kHz**. Root cause: the board's actual Gate_Supply load is only ~35-40mA average (6× UCC21551 VDDx overhead + HPS/Winch/Heater gate-charge delivery) — the inherited 1A target had no relationship to real load and was driving the inductor's physical size unnecessarily. Corrected design current target: 200mA (~5x margin). Saturation current requirement (>1.75A) does not scale down with this correction — it's set by the LM5164's fixed peak current-limit spec (protects against fault conditions, not normal operation), so it remained the binding constraint on inductor selection even after the current-rating fix. Final inductor: Bourns 652-SRN6045TA-330M (33µH, 145mΩ DCR, Isat 2.5A/43% margin, SRF 13MHz, ±20% tolerance) — chosen over a much smaller alternative considered for the 300kHz/68µH case (Würth WE-TPC 3816, 3.8×1.6mm footprint) because that part's Isat margin (1.8A, only 3% over the 1.75A floor) and tolerance (±30%) were both weaker; the safety-critical current-limit margin took priority over footprint size. RRON and the Type-3 ripple network (RA) were recalculated for the new frequency (100kΩ→50.6kΩ, 453kΩ→220kΩ respectively); see `docs/GateSupplyCalculations.md` for the full derivation. One gap knowingly accepted rather than fixed: FB ripple at minimum VIN (15V, the edge of this regulator's operating range) is ~5.3mV, below TI's 12mV recommended floor — present in both the old and new design, and fixing it would cost roughly double the DC regulation error at nominal 48V for a rail that only feeds voltage-tolerant gate driver ICs, so it wasn't judged worth the trade.
- DC-link electrolytic capacitors (KEMET/Yageo A7C0 series) were found undersized on ripple *current* rating, not ripple voltage — the original count passed the ΔV target comfortably while individual capacitors were carrying up to ~3.8x their rated ripple current (see `DCLinkCalculations.md` §3.6 for the general check now added to the methodology). Standardized on a single part across HPS, Winch, and Heater — KEMET `A781MN686M1JLAS035` (68µF/63V, 5.9A rated ripple, 135°C max) — to avoid carrying multiple near-identical capacitor variants in the BOM: 4 units on HPS (~39% ripple current margin, 2.41% ripple voltage), 1 unit each on Winch/Heater (~32% margin, 2.54% ripple voltage). Standardized at 63V across all BOM voltage tiers rather than per-tier, since the 30% margin rule already requires ≥62.4V for the 48V tier
- HPS MOSFET reselected: STL160N4F7 (2.5mΩ RDS(on)) → Infineon ISC011N04NM7V (OptiMOS 7, 1.1mΩ RDS(on)), cutting HPS conduction+switching dissipation from 14.07W to 6.337W (~55%, reflected in `ThermalBudget.md` §4.3/§5/§6). Two other candidates rejected on the RDS(on)×Qg figure-of-merit check (Toshiba TPM1R006PL — clear FOM regression; Infineon BSC010N04LS6/OptiMOS 6 — FOM roughly a wash). Package footprint confirmed against the actual datasheet outline (also modestly smaller than STL160N4F7, not just equal); layout being reworked around the smaller part
- HPS current-sense REF pin was originally tied to a net resolving to the same voltage as VS (found with the earlier INA190A1 amplifier, since replaced by the INA296A2, see the current-sense entry below) — a unidirectional-only special case, not the bidirectional measurement this design needs. Fixed in the schematic: the REF pins (REF1 and REF2 of the INA296A2) are now both driven by a REF3016E (~1.65V) powered from +5V (sheet 05), which the INA296 datasheet (§7.4.3.1) calls the most accurate way to bias the output
- ADC reference (VAREF) net conflict found and resolved during schematic review: the 1.65 V reference net and the XMC4500 VAREF pin carried the same net label (`VREF`), which tied VAREF to 1.65 V while VDDA is 3.3 V. The VADC conversion scale is set by VAREF, so full scale would have been 1.65 V: the bidirectional current-sense outputs (HPS, Winch), centred on 1.65 V, would sit at full scale at zero current, and the TMP235 temperature sensors (0.5 V + 10 mV/°C) would clip above ~115 °C. Resolution: VAREF is fed from the filtered +3.3VA rail (the VDDA supply); the 1.65 V reference only biases the current-sense REF pins. Trade-off accepted: the current-sense zero point is an absolute 1.65 V while the ADC scale now follows the LDO (TLV76133, ±1% at 25 °C, 1.75% over temperature), so the zero-current code can shift by up to ~±35 LSB (~±29 mV, which at 20 V/V is about ±1.4 A on the 1 mΩ HPS shunts and about ±0.3 A on the 5 mΩ Winch shunt) — handled by a firmware zero-current calibration on the bidirectional channels while the bridge is idle. Alternatives considered and not chosen: a dedicated precision 3.3 V reference (REF3333) on VAREF, which keeps the zero point stable but needs a check of the XMC4500 allowed VAREF-versus-VDDA limit (electrical datasheet not available in the project); and a buffered VDDA/2 for the current-sense REF pins so the zero point tracks VAREF
- Gate_Supply (LM5164) SW-node RC snubber footprint is not populated by default in RevA. The LM5164 datasheet shows only an optional snubber (Figure 7-19) with no values, and the loss is significant if populated: P = C·VIN²·fsw dissipated in the snubber resistor, about 0.16 W at 48 V with the ~40 mA average load and about 0.8 W at the 200 mA design target for the 680 pF drawn on the schematic (the drawn values are placeholders). Populate only if bench measurements show a problem: SW peak above about 80 V at 48 V input (pin limit 100 V), undershoot beyond the −1.5 V limit, extra noise on the analog channels or SPI errors with the buck running, or a failed EMC pre-compliance scan. If needed, size it from the measured ring frequency (typically well below 680 pF) and re-check the loss and the resistor power rating
- Global POWER_ENABLE pull-down is 1 kΩ (not 10 kΩ). The net is shared by 6 UCC21551 (EN, internal pull-down 90 kΩ typical), 5 2EDN7533 (ten enable pins with internal 400 kΩ pull-ups to VDD) and 6 DRV8434S (ENABLE, internal pull-up to DVDD). With 10 kΩ the open-line voltage is about 1.8 V, above every device's guaranteed-low level (DRV8434S 0.6 V, UCC21551 0.8 V, 2EDN7533 1.0 V) and below the 2EDN7533's guaranteed-high level, so an unplugged line would be indeterminate instead of disabled. With 1 kΩ it is about 0.34 V nominal (about 0.43 V worst case). Cost: about 3.3 mA from the 3.3 V source, and the high level must stay at 3.3 V (UCC21551 EN limit is VCCI + 0.3 V). The UCC21551 EN pins already carry the 100–1000 pF filter capacitance recommended by TI. Re-check the value if the number of drivers on the net changes
- Freewheel diode for the low-side outputs: Diotec SK36SMA-3G (SMA, 60 V, 3 A Schottky) selected for the Heater output and placed across the load connector pins on the Heater sheet (anode on the switched drain pin, cathode on VDD_IN). Without it, wiring and coil inductance at 5–10 A and 40 kHz would force the 40 V MOSFET into avalanche on every turn-off. Datasheet figures: repetitive peak current 20 A, surge 70/80 A, forward voltage about 0.44 V at 1 A and about 0.7 V at 10 A (typical curves), leakage about 0.35–0.85 mA at 24 V and 100–125 °C, 70 K/W junction to ambient. Estimated diode dissipation for the Heater is about 0.1 W at 1 µH of load inductance and about 0.6 W at 5 µH (assumed values, to be measured). The 48 V tier uses the 100 V siblings of the same family in the same footprint. Commercial grade (not AEC-Q101) is accepted for this project. The LPS outputs use a smaller part, see the next entry
- LPS outputs freewheel diode: Panjit SS1060FL-AU (SOD-123FL, 60 V, 1 A Schottky, AEC-Q101, already in the BOM), one across each of the eight load connectors (anode on the drain pin, cathode on VDD_IN). It is smaller than the SMA part used on the Heater (3.7 mm versus 4.5 mm). It is thermally limited by its 200 K/W minimal-pad figure: inductive PWM loads on the four PWM channels are limited to about 0.5 A average below 50% duty (about 1 A at 50% duty or more); resistive loads, the four GPIO-controlled channels and the glycerin pump (0.25 A maximum) are within limits. The ROHM RB162MM-60 was evaluated and not chosen (lower surge rating, higher forward voltage, no thermal advantage). The 48 V tier needs a 100 V SOD-123FL sibling, not yet identified; the common footprint allows the part to be swapped later
- Input TVS diode on the power input (VDD_IN to GND): ST SM30T-series in SMC (3 kW at 10/1000 µs). SM30T28AY (24 V standoff, breakdown 26.7–29.5 V, clamp 38.9 V at 77 A, 50 V at 660 A for 8/20 µs) for the current 12–24 V BOM; SM30T56AY (48 V standoff, breakdown 53.2–58.8 V, clamp 76.6 V at 38 A, 100 V at 409 A) for the 48 V BOM; both use the same footprint. The number in the part name is the nominal breakdown voltage, not the standoff. The supply is a dedicated buck that must guarantee the maximum input voltage (24.0 V and 48 V respectively) including transients, and overvoltage and regeneration are handled by that supply, so the TVS only covers short bus transients and a live hot-plug. A simple model (ideal step source, 1 µH cable, 980 µF bank, 5 mΩ total series resistance, TVS modelled from the datasheet breakdown and dynamic resistance) gives a hot-plug peak of about 43 V without a TVS and about 36.5 V with the SM30T28AY (TVS peak current about 270 A, inside its rating); for the 48 V variant about 82 V falls to about 77 V. These are estimates that depend on the assumed cable inductance. The 24 V clamp is still above the 30 V LPS MOSFET rating, which it cannot protect. The TVS is unidirectional, so a reversed supply connection relies on the upstream fuse. For bench testing without the buck, connect first and enable the supply afterwards, with the supply's current limit and overvoltage trip set
- UCC21551 dead-time resistors are not populated by default. With the DT pin floating the driver has no hardware interlock (the outputs may overlap), so dead time is provided only by the XMC CCU8 and must be enabled and sized there (the FET turn-off takes about 25–40 ns). The footprint allows a hardware minimum dead time to be added: DT (ns) = 8.6 × RDT (kΩ) + 13, so 10 kΩ gives about 99 ns and 20 kΩ about 185 ns, at about 0.06 W and 0.12 W of body-diode loss at 10 A. The 100 kΩ value drawn on the schematic is a placeholder (about 873 ns and 0.56 W at 10 A if fitted); the POWER_ENABLE enable pin remains the hardware kill for fault conditions. Requirement on the firmware: set the CCU8 dead time just above the FET turn-off time (about 25–40 ns) and keep it as short as the gate network allows, because the body diode conducts during the whole dead time (loss per bridge about 4 × fPWM × tdead × VSD × I) and `ThermalBudget.md` does not include this loss yet; the thermal re-check uses a conservative 200 ns
- Winch (and HPS) gate networks follow the TI recommended UCC21551 network: turn-on through the 2 Ω RON, turn-off through RON in parallel with 2 Ω ROFF in series with a Schottky diode (about 1 Ω plus the diode drop). Turn-off is therefore faster than turn-on by design. `ThermalBudget.md` assumes 2 Ω in both directions, so its Winch and HPS switching losses are slightly conservative
- External NTC temperature sensors, each in a divider from +3.3 V (pull-up, then a 1 kΩ series resistor to the ADC): the two HPS motor channels use Eaton NRNE104H3950B1H (10 kΩ, B 3950 K, ±3 %, rated −40 to +125 °C, which is enough because it measures the motor casing) with a 1.5 kΩ pull-up; the heater channel uses Eaton NRBG105F3950B1F (100 kΩ, B 3950 K, ±1 %, glass, rated −40 to +300 °C, bare Dumet leads that need a crimp or weld rather than solder) with a 3.3 kΩ pull-up. The heater runs in a closed current loop with a water–glycerin mixture, so the sensor only monitors and protects: normal operation stays at or below 200 °C (setpoint about 150–200 °C), and the 300 °C measurement range is there for over-temperature protection only (trip level to be chosen, about 250–260 °C, below the glycerin decomposition range). The heater part's resistance does not follow B = 3950 K at high temperature (478 Ω at 200 °C in the datasheet table against about 745 Ω from the formula), so firmware must use the R–T table. On the heater channel a short reads as over-temperature and disables the heater, like an over-temperature reading
- No series resistors on the LPS gate-driver inputs (2EDN7533FXTMA1 INA/INB), to keep the component count down. The inputs tolerate −12 V to +22 V (absolute maximum) with no current through the ESD structure at negative levels, so a series resistor is not needed for protection. The thresholds are LV-TTL (about 1.2 V low, 1.9–2.3 V high) with 0.9 V typical hysteresis, and a pulse shorter than the minimum input pulse width (6 ns typical, 10 ns maximum) does not change the output, so ringing or a coupled spike inside the hysteresis band does not toggle it. Every input has a 10 kΩ pull-down, so the inputs idle low while the MCU pins float at reset. The only long run is the LPS channel on the left-edge MCU pin; if ringing shows up in testing, soften the MCU pad edge first (pad driver strength and edge mode in the port configuration, no extra part) before adding a resistor
- Current-sense amplifier replaced: INA190A1 (25 V/V) → TI INA296A2IDGKR (20 V/V, VSSOP-8) on all four channels (HPS A, HPS B, Winch, Heater). The INA190A1 (about 45 kHz bandwidth, 0.3 V/µs slew rate, about 30 µs settling) was too slow for PWM-synchronous low-side sampling at 40 kHz: the shunt carries current only during the on-phase, so an end-of-pulse sample under-reads at low duty (about 51 % of the true value at 10 % duty). The INA296 gives 1.1 MHz bandwidth, 8 V/µs, 1 µs settling to 1 %, offset ±10 µV max and gain error ±0.01 % max. Candidates rejected: TSC200 (unidirectional, ±2.5 mV offset, 2 µs settling) and MAX4376 (unidirectional high-side, ±3–7 % error); the INA790 was rejected because its gain is not fixed. 20 V/V was chosen as the compromise: 30 V/V does not exist in a fast fixed-gain part and would clip the Winch and Heater, 10 V/V halves the resolution. Sensitivity is 20 mV/A on the 1 mΩ HPS shunts (linear range about ±72 A) and 100 mV/A on the 5 mΩ Winch and Heater shunts (Winch ±14.5 A). The SOT-23-8 versions (A2, B2) were out of stock, so the VSSOP-8 INA296A2IDGKR was selected (same pinout, larger footprint). HPS and Winch: both REF pins on the 1.65 V reference. Heater (unidirectional): both REF pins on GND, so the output rests at 0 V and rises with current. Pin 4 (NC) is tied to GND and each supply pin has a 100 nF capacitor. Details in `CurrentSenseCalculations.md`
- `XMC_RDY` (ready flag to the MainControlBoard) is an open-drain output, active low, with a 4.7 kΩ pull-up to +3.3 V. The P14/P15 pads are input-only and cannot be used for it
- RC servo signal lines have a 330 Ω series resistor each (six in total), between the MCU pin and the connector, to limit the current into the MCU pin if a servo cable is shorted or hot-plugged and to damp ringing on the cable. No pull-down is fitted: servos pull their own signal input low, and 330 Ω is small enough not to disturb a standard 3.3 V servo input
- `GNDDetect` (debug probe present) uses the internal pull-up of the MCU pin instead of an external resistor, to save one component
- Auxiliary supply inputs: the +5 V and +6 V lines each have a fuse on the power input sheet, Yageo JB12S series (1206 slow-blow, rated 63 Vdc up to 10 A): 1 A (JB12S1001R) on +5 V and 2 A (JB12S1002R) on +6 V. The footprint also accepts a higher-rated fuse of the same series if the loads grow
- Encoder receiver outputs (ENC_1_OUTA, ENC_1_OUTB, ENC_2_OUTA, ENC_2_OUTB) have a 22 Ω series resistor each (four in total), drawn on the MCU sheet, to damp edge ringing and reflections on the trace from the ISL32273E to the MCU, limit the current into the MCU pin and reduce coupling into neighbouring analog traces. The value matches the 22 Ω already used at the stepper driver SDO outputs. The added time constant (about 0.2 ns with a 10 pF pin) is negligible against encoder frequencies of 0.5 to 2.2 MHz at 32 500 RPM. Placement relative to the receiver and the MCU is decided in the layout

## Known Issues

- Board-half thermal asymmetry was a live concern while HPS_A/B and HPS_C/D were planned adjacent at 14.07W dissipation; substantially less severe now that the MOSFET reselection dropped HPS to 6.337W, but the via-density/copper-spreading mitigation still hasn't been formally verified
- 3.3 V logic LDO (TLV76133): the reverse-current / back-driven case (TLV761 datasheet §7.1.3) is deliberately not addressed in RevA — there is no reverse-current protection across the regulator. It applies if the 5 V input collapses while the output capacitance (~10 µF) still holds charge, or if the 3.3 V rail is held up through external connections (debug probe, MainControlBoard link) while 5 V is off. Judged low risk at this capacitance and with the rail's continuous load; revisit if any connected board can drive the rail with 5 V off (possible fix: a reverse-protection diode from output to input)
- Input LC resonance damping on the VDD_IN bus (LM5164 datasheet §7.3) is not addressed in RevA. The bulk input capacitance is the DC-link bank on the same net (hybrid-polymer electrolytics plus ceramics, about 4–5 mΩ ESR for the bank), which is low-ESR and does not damp resonance, and no dedicated damping element is provided. The Gate_Supply sheet still describes a "moderate ESR" input capacitor for this purpose, which does not match the implementation. Hot-plug overshoot on the bus is limited only by the input TVS diode and by connecting before enabling the supply (see the TVS entry once placed on the power input sheet)
- LPS load connectors are mirrored in pairs by design to ease routing, so the pin polarity alternates between outputs: on outputs 1, 3, 5 and 7, pin 1 is the switched side and pin 2 is VDD_IN; on outputs 2, 4, 6 and 8 it is the reverse. A polarized load wired with the wrong convention would be reversed. Mitigation: mark the polarity on the silkscreen (layout task) and follow it when making the harnesses
- The stepper driver (DRV8434S) is limited to 48 V recommended and 50 V absolute maximum on VM, so it cannot give the 1.3× voltage margin (`ElectricalRequirements.md` §3.5) in the 48 V BOM tier; the input TVS would clamp at 76.6 V there. Accepted as a limitation: it is fine in the 12–24 V BOM (TVS clamp 38.9 V), and operation near 48 V needs extra care, especially hot plugging, where a lower supply voltage tolerates spikes that a 48 V supply does not
- Encoder inputs (ISL32273E): the fail-safe bias footprints (pull-up to +5 V and pull-down to GND per line) are not populated by default, which is valid for an always-driven RS-422 encoder (Renesas TB509 §4.1). Without them, with the 120 Ω termination fitted and the encoder disconnected or unpowered, the differential input is about 0 V and the receiver output is undefined (the ISL32273E is fail-safe only for open inputs), so a disconnected encoder cannot be detected and may produce spurious counts. The 3.3 kΩ value drawn on the schematic is not sufficient if populated: it gives only about 89 mV against the 200 mV threshold plus 50 mV noise margin that TB509 requires. About 1 kΩ gives about 270 mV (TB509 Eq. 7, 4.75–5.25 V supply) at about 2.4 mA per resistor, with the termination impedance about 113 Ω
- The +5 V encoder supply goes to the encoder connectors with no current limit or fuse, and the same 5 V rail feeds the 3.3 V LDO and the 1.65 V reference. A short or overload in an encoder cable could pull down the logic supply. Accepted as a limitation of RevA; a resettable fuse or load switch per connector would be the fix
- Temperature sensors (TMP235A4): no supply bypass capacitor on VDD in RevA, although the datasheet strongly recommends one (0.1 µF, plus 100 Ω in series with the supply in noisy environments). Omitted on purpose: the sensor output only needs to be accurate to a degree or so, its supply sensitivity is small (line regulation 0.1 °C/V maximum), and firmware will average (oversample) 4–16 ADC samples per reading. If readings turn out noisy, add the 0.1 µF capacitor (and the 100 Ω) at the sensor supply pin
- External NTC temperature channels (HPS motors and heater): no filter capacitor at the ADC pin in RevA, although a 100 nF capacitor after the series resistor was planned. Omitted on purpose to save a component: temperature changes slowly, and firmware oversamples 4–16 samples without PWM synchronization, which averages out random noise. The cost is that the series resistor plus the divider impedance (up to about 2.3 kΩ) drives the ADC sampling capacitor directly, so these channels need a sufficiently long ADC sample time, and pickup from the motor cables is not filtered in hardware. If the readings turn out noisy or offset, add the capacitor (100 nF, X7R, close to the ADC pin)
- Limit switch inputs (six 2-pin connectors, 10 kΩ pull-up to +3.3 V each): no ESD protection and no series resistor or debounce filter in RevA, to keep the component count down. A bounce is harmless because the first edge stops the motor in the interrupt routine (the stop is latched and must not be released by a later edge). Cable pickup can cause nuisance stops, which is the fail-safe direction; add a series resistor and capacitor if it shows up in testing.

## Lessons Learned

- Ripple *voltage* passing the target does not mean a DC-link capacitor bank is safe — each capacitor's individual ripple *current* must be checked against its own datasheet rating separately; the two can diverge badly (caught late in this revision on the HPS A7C0 bank)
- RDS(on)×Qg is a real figure of merit, roughly constant within a given silicon process/generation — a lower-RDS(on) MOSFET candidate should be checked on this product, not just on headline RDS(on), and raw datasheet switching-time (ns) comparisons are unreliable across parts tested at different external gate resistances
- Connector wire-gauge rating must be cross-checked explicitly against the actual wire gauge in use, not assumed to still match once either one changes independently — a documented "16-14 AWG" connector silently ended up serving 18 AWG wire because the two facts were established in separate decisions and never compared side by side
- Current-return polygon reshaping: cutting path *length* is close to a free win (space + lower resistance/inductance); cutting path *width* needs a full ampacity check, since it reduces the current-carrying cross-section
- No formal via-ampacity table exists yet for this project; working estimate used so far is ~1–1.5A per via (IPC-2221-style) for the standard/power via class
- Net labels connect across sheets by name, so a generic name such as `VREF` can silently join two unrelated nets. Reference and supply nets should carry names that state their voltage and purpose (e.g. 1.65 V current-sense bias versus the ADC reference). Also, the XMC4500 VADC input range (0 V to VDDA) is the allowed pin voltage, not the conversion scale — that is set by VAREF
